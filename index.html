About

From classical texts to health and society

I hold a Bachelor's and a Master's degree from the Department of Sanskrit at the University of Dhaka. Working closely with texts taught me to read carefully for belief, meaning and tradition — and led me to ask how those same cultural ideas shape everyday decisions about health.

I am now preparing for master's and doctoral study, and my work sits where six areas meet: medical sociology, health-seeking behaviour, women's health, chronic disease awareness, social norms, and quantitative and qualitative methods. In Bangladesh, I study how fatalism, gender norms, family decision-making and access to care shape when — and whether — people seek help for illness.

Read the full abstract

Bangladesh is facing growing concerns about chronic diseases, including diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and cancer, particularly breast cancer. Although many chronic diseases are preventable and some are treatable, cultural beliefs, socioeconomic disparities, and unequal access to healthcare contribute to low levels of awareness and delayed diagnosis, particularly in rural communities. This research project investigates how cultural mindsets influence awareness of chronic diseases and cancer in Bangladesh, with a particular focus on breast cancer as a major health concern among women.

This mixed-methods study involved a quantitative survey of 500 people living in Dhaka, Barisal, and Sylhet, as well as qualitative interviews with 50 respondents. The results indicate prominent urban–rural differences: urban respondents demonstrated higher levels of general knowledge about chronic diseases and breast cancer, attended regular medical check-ups more frequently, and relied more on formal healthcare services. Rural respondents, by contrast, exhibited more fatalistic attitudes, greater reliance on traditional healers, and lower levels of breast cancer awareness and knowledge of breast self-examination.

This research highlights the urgent need for culturally responsive health interventions that accommodate local customs, address gender inequality, and improve the availability of healthcare services. Such interventions could be delivered through mobile health clinics, mass media, and community health workers. Policymakers should reduce disparities by developing strategies that reflect local cultural realities and promote the early diagnosis of chronic diseases and cancer.

Keywords: cultural beliefs, chronic diseases, breast cancer awareness, health-seeking behaviour, Bangladesh
